# Editorial calibration

Examples of the maintainer's editorial taste. The original Beautifully deck and the current deck live in the WhoCards repository (`packages/decks/src/decks/beautifully.questions.json` and `packages/decks/src/pool/questions.json`); the comparisons below are a selection that teaches taste, with exact English text and paragraph breaks flattened in tables. Interpretations of historical edits are inferences; the maintainer's direct feedback takes precedence.

## What the revisions teach

| ID  | Beautifully                                                              | Current                                                       | Editorial implication                                                       |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 25  | Do you believe in religion, and why?                                     | What is your relationship with religion?                      | A lived relationship gives more room than defending a position.             |
| 24  | What is one thing about yourself you feel that you should be working on? | What's a recurring conflict in your life?                     | Notice experience rather than begin with a verdict about what needs fixing. |
| 17  | For what in your life do you feel most grateful for at the moment?       | What is something you are grateful for at the moment?         | Let someone choose an answer without ranking all possibilities.             |
| 46  | What's the best advice you've been given?                                | What's the best advice you've been given? Who gave it to you? | A useful follow-up opens a relationship behind the answer.                  |

The current deck also includes "How do you like to be loved?", "When do you feel alone?", and "How do you feel about death?" Direct, brief, emotionally demanding questions remain welcome.

These changes show a range of useful moves, not a rule that every question must request a story, avoid a yes/no opening, or become lighthearted. A shared ID sometimes means the subject was replaced entirely. English comparisons do not establish naturalness in other translations.

## Maintainer feedback on generated questions · 2026-09-08

Frame: being a parent (no time constraint). Two questions received 5/5 with no wording correction:

> What part of being a parent takes more out of you than people seem to notice?

> What is a parenting rule you were sure about before you became a parent? How do you feel about it now?

Other feedback establishes these preferences:

- A shared parent-and-child experience was a good subject, but "a small thing" and the long setup added restriction and filler. "Something" leaves the scale open.
- Realizing that you take yourself too seriously was a good direction. Requiring the child to have made the parent laugh added an unnecessary event.
- For trust, the maintainer preferred something the parent is learning to trust the child with over a generic easy/hard comparison.
- Questions about a surprising life change or a memorable conversation should allow that there may not be one. Asking "Who did you get to see a different side of?" wrongly presumed an event.
- In "What mattered a lot…", remove "a lot." In a question about a moment worth photographing, remove "ordinary." Keep useful meaning while letting the answerer choose significance.

The next revision was rejected before review because four out of five questions began "Is there…". The lesson is to preserve openness through different kinds of invitations, not to insert the same hedge everywhere. These feedback examples are evidence for editorial judgment, not templates to copy across a batch.

## Follow-up taste test · 2026-09-08

The maintainer rated all five parenting and all five difficult-day questions 5/5, including direct questions about learning, rethinking an expectation, and an unseen burden. Directness or a plausible frame-grounded experience is not itself a failure. Judge whether the question puts an unwanted interpretation on the person, rather than imposing a blanket ban on assumptions.

The specific corrections were:

- A writing prompt about an old idea of home and new forms of home was too broad: two full subjects. A brief follow-up on the same scene can still work.
- An instruction-only route-observation exercise was rejected as "not a question." Writing prompts should include a question unless an exercise is requested.
- "What helps you feel comfortable speaking up" implied discomfort the user had not supplied.
- For a theme song, "what would it be?" was clearer than "what would you nominate?" For an afternoon together, a direct "if" question needed no "Imagine" setup.

The dance-performance and jungle-survival cards are excluded from positive calibration. They remain in the existing-deck snapshot for duplicate checking.
