# Generate Questions for The Third Relationship

Use this prompt when preparing the Questions. The outline keeps their purpose and timing; the `whocards-questions` skill supplies the wording. This prompt has not been run yet.

```text
Use the whocards-questions skill to prepare Questions for The Third Relationship B2B workshop.

Read soul.md and docs/workshops/foundations.md for the intent first, then docs/workshops/first-prototype.md and docs/workshops/facilitation-research.md for the working design and supporting guidance. The foundations contain the latest intent; the agenda's advance-reflection instructions still need review against the usual card ritual. Write spoken card Questions for first hearing on the answerer's turn. Consult docs/workshops/b2b-outline.md for existing-team adaptations. Follow the skill's authoring and review process. If the skill is unavailable, say so rather than claiming to have used it.

Prioritize the first prototype: a 90-minute workshop for people who are not an existing team. Participants bring different workplace experiences and levels of AI use. The primary intention is meaningful human connection and more conscious working relationships as AI becomes part of work. Connection can be worthwhile in itself, and discovery remains open. Seeing processes more clearly is secondary; AI tips are welcome incidental learning. Slowing down supports attention and listening; pressure to keep up is one possible theme. Use the moments below as provisional design context to review against this intent.

Prepare one recommended Question or invitation and one alternative for each moment:

- General WhoCards round: help people meet beyond their job titles. Allow about three minutes per person in pairs, within a 12-minute block. Avoid requiring intimate disclosure. The hosts can also use a candidate from this group for their brief opening answers.
- AI-focused round: connect how a person turns to, trusts, questions, or avoids AI with their interactions with people. Allow private reflection and time for both partners within a 15-minute block. People who don't use AI must be able to participate fully.
- Transition into the core exercise: a brief private reflection invitation that helps someone recall an experience showing a connection between their relationship with AI and a human interaction. The core allows each partner eight minutes with their own experience; include helpful and uncomfortable experiences. Keep it optional; the outline provides a fictional case as a fallback.
- Closing reflection: help people name what they want to take back to work. Answers should fit a brief voluntary share within a three-minute block. A separate three-minute workshop feedback form follows; this Question should focus on what became clearer for the participant, without requiring a new task or commitment.

Keep the language natural and easy to understand aloud. Allow excitement, uncertainty, disagreement, and disinterest without steering people toward a preferred view of AI. Don't assume AI is a person, that adoption is desirable, or that someone has authority to change their workplace. Avoid asking for identifying company or client details, judging absent colleagues' motives, or reporting a partner's story.

For each recommendation, briefly explain its purpose in the sequence and any participation concern the hosts should consider. Suggest an existing-team adaptation only where the wording needs to change, accounting for colleagues and managers being in the room.

Distinguish newly generated Questions from verified existing Deck content. Keep alternatives as preparation choices, not extra rounds. Don't expand the agenda or edit the website.

Return the candidates for review before adding selected wording to the outline.
```
