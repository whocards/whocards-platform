# WhoCards workshop foundations

This document captures the intent clarified with Avi. Read it when designing a WhoCards workshop, preparing its Question brief, evaluating an activity, or writing its landing page. The lasting brand principles live in [soul.md](../../soul.md); each workshop's outline records its particular design choices.

## What carries across offerings

Meaningful human connection is the core brand purpose. A workshop gives that purpose a setting and a subject to explore together. An encounter can be worthwhile in itself, including between strangers. Useful learning and practical changes may grow from it.

Design for honest self-expression and attentive listening. Protect the usual card ritual: the participant encounters the Question when their turn begins. Choose spoken Questions that make sense on first hearing and leave room for an unrehearsed answer. Give any separate reflection activity its own purpose rather than treating advance preparation as an automatic improvement to the game.

The intention can be clear while discovery remains open. Participants decide what is meaningful in their experience and what they want to share. They may name that discovery during the session, in feedback, or later. A polished insight, agreement, personal disclosure, or new commitment is not a condition of success.

## Intent for The Third Relationship

> Create space for meaningful human connection and more conscious relationships at work, as AI becomes part of how we work together.
>
> Through honest sharing and attentive listening, we explore our relationships with people and AI, bringing greater awareness to what we trust, what we value, and how we choose to work.

The third relationship offers a perspective on how the way someone relates to AI can affect their relationships with other people. Participants can also examine their direct relationship with AI. They need not regard AI as a person or find that the same framing explains every experience.

### Priorities

| Role             | Intention                                                                                                                  |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary          | Meaningful human connection and more conscious understanding of relationships involving people, work, and AI.              |
| How we work      | Focus, slowing down, honest sharing, and attentive listening.                                                              |
| Secondary        | Seeing familiar processes and practices more clearly, including whether they are dependable and serve the people involved. |
| Welcome learning | AI knowledge, tips, and techniques that arise usefully from the exchange.                                                  |

Other workshops may have explicit learning objectives of their own. Carry the connection principles into those offers while defining their particular purpose; this hierarchy describes The Third Relationship.

“More conscious” means seeing a relationship or choice more clearly. It can support keeping a sound process, defining a boundary, trying something useful, or deciding against a change. Newness alone adds no value. Greater closeness to AI is not the goal.

Healthier relationships are a direction to explore through concrete experiences such as trust, choice, being heard, and being able to discuss expectations. Avoid defining health as greater AI use, unanimous enthusiasm, or a promise that one workshop will resolve workplace problems.

Connection in the room is an immediate opportunity. Carrying that experience into working life is an aspiration, potentially supported by the AI at Work cards. People outside the room retain their own perspective and agency.

## What this means for landing pages

Lead with the human invitation and describe what participation involves. Match the audience and practical details to the actual offer. Explain the opportunity for connection and reflection without guaranteeing an emotional response, a transformation, or a workplace result.

Keep the language open to enthusiasm, concern, and uncertainty. The page should help someone recognize whether the experience is for them. A participant can be welcome before they have a settled view of AI or a problem to fix.

## What belongs in the question skill

The existing `whocards-questions` skill already supports honest expression, active listening, and depth without a required conclusion. A focused future update should add only the missing implications:

- Connection can be the complete value of an answer; a clever insight or useful lesson is optional.
- In a spoken card game, assume the answerer encounters the Question on their turn. It should work without preview, research, or a rehearsed response. Preserve the skill's separate support for journaling and other formats.
- For workplace AI frames, invite awareness and considered choices. A sound existing practice or a boundary is as valid a discovery as a new use of AI.

Keep workshop agendas, timing, and commercial positioning in workshop documents. The skill's portable mission reference can carry the general principles; its writing guidance should contain only what changes Question authoring. These additions are planned separately in [#296: proposed skill v0.3](https://github.com/whocards/whocards-platform/issues/296), after the existing v0.2 work. They have not been applied to the skill.

For the conference prototype, the current question is what to invite people into from that connection, in service of the intent and with sharing appropriate to the setting.

Discuss that invitation before revising the agenda, then examine one design choice at a time. For each choice, identify what human purpose it serves and what it might crowd out. Develop Questions and landing-page copy once the intended experience is clear enough to guide them.

The existing 90-minute prototype is a working hypothesis. Its advance-reflection instructions need a deliberate review against the card ritual above.
