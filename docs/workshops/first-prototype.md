# The Third Relationship: first prototype

**90-minute conference workshop · Draft for discussion and feedback**

Facilitated by Avi and Tamas. Participants bring different workplace experiences and levels of AI use; they aren't an existing team.

## The intent

Create space for meaningful human connection and more conscious relationships at work, as AI becomes part of how we work together.

Through honest sharing and attentive listening, we explore how the way we relate to AI can affect our relationships with other people. Connection is worthwhile in itself. The hope is that participants carry some of that awareness and connection back into working life.

Slowing down supports the experience. Looking at processes more clearly is secondary; useful AI tips are welcome. Increased AI use or greater closeness to AI aren't goals. Participants choose what they share and what they discover.

## The 90-minute flow

| Time  | Duration | What happens                                                                    |
| ----- | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 00–08 | 8 min    | Welcome, grounding, introductions to the facilitators and WhoCards.             |
| 08–28 | 20 min   | General and AI-focused Question rounds in pairs, with brief whole-group shares. |
| 28–38 | 10 min   | Avi's story, one audience share, then Tamas's story.                            |
| 38–45 | 7 min    | A few slides and conversation introducing the third relationship.               |
| 45–50 | 5 min    | Break.                                                                          |
| 50–75 | 25 min   | Small-group activity about AI at work; the format is still open.                |
| 75–90 | 15 min   | Reflection, closing shares, thanks, and a short feedback form.                  |

Timing is provisional; each block includes instructions and transitions.

## How the experience develops

**Start with connection.** Question rounds give each person two minutes to answer, with one minute between rounds to finish and swap partners. People first see their Question when it's their turn. Sharing is voluntary, and participants speak for themselves rather than retelling a partner's story. One or two brief group shares bring attention back to the room and facilitators.

The opening currently allows for one general round and one AI-focused round; the final number is open. Two paired rounds plus the partner transition take nine minutes, leaving the rest of the 20-minute block for instructions and group shares. Questions will be prepared later using the whocards-questions skill.

**Continue through personal stories.** Avi shares how he decided, less than a week before his birthday, that he wanted a mobile app in the App Store for his birthday, and made it happen. The feeling: "magic pure fucking magic." An audience member shares next, followed by Tamas, whose story is still to be chosen. These stories lead into the concepts and how AI is changing working relationships.

**Explore together.** The activity should bring people into a slightly larger group, where they first share with each other and then contribute or create something together. It may involve a simple form submission or generating something with AI. The task, group size, and output remain undecided; all parts must fit within 25 minutes.

**Leave room for discovery.** Close with reflection and a few voluntary shares, without requiring an action plan or a polished takeaway. Reserve the final three minutes for a short, optional workshop feedback form.

## Feedback we would value

- Whether the intent and connection between the opening conversations and AI at work feel clear.
- Whether the balance of conversation, stories, facilitator input, and group activity feels right.
- Ideas for a simple group activity that depends on listening and sharing first.
- Anything missing that would help this work in a conference setting.
