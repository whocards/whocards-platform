# The Third Relationship: B2B workshop outline

Status: working draft. The first prototype is for participants who are **not an existing team**. Keep the existing-team version as a separate variant of the same workshop.

Supporting notes: [facilitation research](facilitation-research.md). The timings and exercises below remain proposals to test.

## Intent

Create a shared table where people can explore how AI is changing their working relationships.

Avi's starting image: we aren't sitting around a new machine and figuring it out together. People encounter AI through distributed experiments and competing claims about the right way to use it. This workshop gives people a place to compare experiences and examine what those experiences mean for working with others.

Working definition to test with participants: **Your relationship with AI changes how you relate to the people you work with.** Here, “relationship with AI” means how someone uses, trusts, questions, or avoids it. Participants don't have to regard AI as a person for this framing to be useful.

The opening keeps the human emphasis: a moment to arrive, a reminder of the world beyond our screens, and curiosity about the people in the room. Make room for concern rather than opening with a promise that AI will be harmless.

## Two variants

|                      | Mixed group: first prototype                                                                                        | Existing team                                                                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Participants         | People bringing different workplace experiences; they need not share an employer or know one another.               | Colleagues who share work and will continue working together.                   |
| Intended outcome     | Recognize something about one's relationship with AI and its possible effect on an interaction with another person. | Understand differences within the team and agree on one change to try together. |
| Core material        | An anonymized participant example or a clearly fictional workplace situation.                                       | A shared work situation that the people involved agree to discuss.              |
| Closing output       | A clearer understanding, with an optional conversation, boundary, or change to take back to work.                   | A team experiment with an owner and review date.                                |
| Limit of the session | Participants can explore other perspectives, but can't settle issues or make agreements for absent colleagues.      | Team decisions depend on who is present and what authority they have.           |

## Existing-team variant

Use the 90-minute flow in the [first-prototype outline](first-prototype.md), with the following changes. The break remains five minutes and the closing includes a three-minute feedback form.

Before the session, speak with the sponsor about the team's situation, who will attend, and which decisions the group can make. Agree on what will be shared afterward. Ask about reporting relationships so the pair arrangements and questions account for differences in seniority.

During the opening, explain those agreements to everyone. Provide quiet writing time before group discussion, allow passes, and ask leaders to listen before offering their interpretation. Use voluntary, general themes when personal examples would expose someone unnecessarily.

In the AI round, ask what colleagues wish others on this team understood about their experience. Gather differences without pushing for agreement.

For the core work, choose a real shared situation with the consent of those involved. Examine what each person knew and expected. If the room can't discuss a live issue constructively, use a fictional example and record the unresolved issue for a suitable later conversation.

Close by choosing one team experiment, with a named owner, start date, and review date. Write down how the team will judge whether it helped and preserve unresolved disagreements. If the team lacks authority to act, identify a proposal and who will take it forward.

Keep the final three minutes for the same individual feedback form. Explain in advance what feedback, if any, will be shared with the sponsor, following the session's reporting agreement.
