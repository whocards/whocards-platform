# WhoCards @ Work — B2B Business Plan

_Prepared 2026-07-04. Reframes the AI-at-work wedge as **one complete B2B offering**: a single
ladder that runs from **high-touch human facilitation** (Avi or a coworker in the room) at the top
to **low-touch self-serve tools + questions** at the bottom. Builds on
[`ai-at-work.md`](./ai-at-work.md) (competitor thesis, 2026-06-16) and
[`../growth/03-growth-strategy.md`](../growth/03-growth-strategy.md) (Lever 5). This doc adds what
those were missing: **market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM), unit economics, revenue projections, and derived
marketing + dev plans.**_

> **One-line thesis.** Companies are spending record amounts on AI but adoption stalls on **people,
> not tech**. Nobody owns the _honest-conversation layer_ of AI adoption. WhoCards already is a
> honest-conversation engine. We productize that engine into a ladder — a $0 deck at the bottom, a
> facilitated session with a human at the top — where the free/cheap end feeds leads to the
> expensive end, and the expensive end produces the proof (logos, case studies) that sells the
> cheap end.

## 1. The reframe: one offering, a service↔product spectrum

Avi's instinct is the right structure. Instead of "a landing page plus a deck," treat it as **a
single product line with a high-touch pole and a low-touch pole**, and everything in between is a
rung on the same ladder.

```
 HIGH-TOUCH ($$$, low scale, high margin-of-proof)     ← "me or a coworker facilitates"
   ▲
   │  Tier 4  Facilitator network / train-the-facilitator + multi-session programs
   │  Tier 3  Facilitated team session (Avi/coworker runs it live)   ── the SERVICE
   │  Tier 2  WhoCards for Teams — SaaS: org accounts, custom AI decks, cadence, recap
   │  Tier 1  "Navigating AI Together" — paid deck + facilitator guide (physical + digital)
   │  Tier 0  "The AI Check-In" — free deck + landing (lead magnet)  ── the TOOLS
   ▼
 LOW-TOUCH ($/$0, high scale, high gross margin)        ← "just a bunch of tools + questions"
```

Two economic engines, deliberately coupled:

| Pole                      | What it is                                    | Scales by                                                | Margin                                                           | Job in the business                                                          |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **High-touch** (Tier 3–4) | A human facilitates an honest AI conversation | Adding facilitators (you → coworker → certified network) | High revenue/day, but **capped by human time** until productized | Cash now, credibility, case studies, logos, discovery of the real buyer pain |
| **Low-touch** (Tier 0–2)  | Self-serve deck, SaaS, custom question packs  | Software (near-zero marginal cost)                       | 85–90% gross                                                     | The compounding, defensible, recurring business                              |

**The flywheel:** Tier 0 (free) captures L&D/manager emails at scale → some convert to Tier 1/2
self-serve → the hottest accounts pull us into a Tier 3 facilitated session → that session becomes a
case study + a Tier 2 team license + a referral → which makes the next Tier 0 landing convert
better. **Services on top de-risk and sell the product underneath; the product underneath makes the
services repeatable.** This is the productized-service model (start as a service to learn the buyer,
harden into a product), not a consultancy and not a lone SaaS.

## 3. Market sizing (TAM / SAM / SOM)

No single clean market exists — we sit at the intersection of several. Adjacent 2026 markets, for
orientation (not our claim):

| Adjacent market                                       | 2026 size                              | Note                                         |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Global corporate training / L&D                       | **~$380B** (→ ~$510B by 2030)          | The ocean we swim in                         |
| AI-powered corporate training                         | **~$7.5B** (19% CAGR)                  | Mostly _capability_ (LMS), not _willingness_ |
| Change-management / digital-transformation consulting | **~$100B**                             | Top-down, expensive, episodic                |
| AI consulting                                         | **~$14B** (26% CAGR)                   | Technical, not human-layer                   |
| Corporate team-building services                      | **~$1.5–3B** (narrow) to ~$50B (broad) | Adjacent budget line                         |

Our category — **"the honest-conversation / willingness layer of AI adoption"** — is a _new
micro-category carved from these_. Sized three ways, honestly (these are planning estimates with
wide ranges, not forecasts):

- **TAM ≈ $5–10B/yr.** Every org globally actively rolling out AI that needs the human/willingness
  layer. Bottoms-up: ~1–2M companies worldwide with 200+ employees; even $2–5k/yr each on
  AI-conversation tooling + facilitation ⇒ $2–10B. Cross-check top-down: ~2–3% of the ~$380B
  learning spend is plausibly "AI adoption, human side" ⇒ ~$8–11B. Call it **~$5–10B**.
- **SAM ≈ $0.3–1B/yr.** What WhoCards can actually serve: mid-market (200–2,000 employees),
  reachable via PLG + LinkedIn + SEO, in English + our 14 localized languages (a genuine edge —
  distributed/global teams, few competitors localized). ~200–400k reachable orgs × blended
  $1–3k/yr ACV.
- **SOM ≈ $0.5–6M ARR in 36 months.** What a small team can realistically capture: **hundreds to a
  couple thousand paying teams/orgs** plus a facilitation services line. Concretely a 3-year target
  band of ~500–2,000 paying teams at $1–3k + 50–200 facilitated engagements. This is the number the
  plan is actually built to hit.

**The point of the sizing:** the category is big enough that a focused wedge can build a real
business, but the win condition is **owning a narrow, well-named micro-category** ("the AI Check-In
/ WhoCards @ Work"), not capturing a slice of the $380B ocean.

## 4. Competition & the gap we own

Four clusters; our opening is the empty middle (full landscape in
[`ai-at-work.md`](./ai-at-work.md) §3). Refreshed:

- **A — Generic connection decks** (We're Not Really Strangers, TableTopics Team, Actually Curious).
  ~$18–30 retail, one-time novelty. **WNRS is the category's cautionary tale:** viral
  consumer/TikTok brand, est. **~$4.8M revenue in 2024, down ~10–20%**, and projected to keep
  declining — because one-time consumer decks have **no recurring revenue and no B2B motion.** They
  don't touch AI. This is the trap we avoid by going B2B + recurring.
- **B — Facilitation/workshop decks** (SessionLab, Synergy Stack). Professional tools, higher WTP,
  but method-focused, not emotion-focused, not AI-specific.
- **C — AI-literacy tools/LMS** (Go1, SAP, AI Tinkerers' cards). Timely, but they teach
  **mechanics** (what's a prompt/token). They make people _capable_, not _willing_. **We pair with
  them, we don't compete** — capability + willingness.
- **D — AI adoption workshops & change-mgmt consulting** ($3–12k/half-to-full-day workshops;
  executive sessions $5–20k; bootcamps $8–40k; big consulting $8k–$150k). Top-down, expensive,
  episodic. The honest peer-level conversation doesn't happen in a $75k exec session.

**The gap = AI-specific + emotionally honest + sellable as both a low-touch product _and_ a
high-touch service.** Nobody occupies it. Defensibility vs. an incumbent fast-follow (WNRS could
ship an "AI edition"): our **multilingual engine + the Tier 2 recurring/custom platform** (issue
#45) is a software moat a card company can't cheaply match. **Move on Tier 2 before the category
crowds.**

## 5. The offering ladder — what we sell, and for how much

Pricing anchored to the comps in §4. Each rung reuses tech we already have.

### Tier 0 — "The AI Check-In" — FREE deck + landing — _the lead magnet_ **(ship now)**

- 30–40 prompts a manager runs in a ~20-min team check-in. Live on the `/play` engine at
  `/play/ai-at-work`; landing at `/ai-at-work` (both built). Email capture live.
- **Price: $0.** Goal = traffic + emails + virality, not revenue.
- **Status:** built. Gated only on the HITL question-variant pick (PR #144) + an OG card + funnel
  instrumentation.

### Tier 1 — "Navigating AI Together" — paid deck + facilitator guide — _first revenue_

- 100–150 cards in acts (_Name the Fear → Map the Work → Redesign the Role → Team Norms for AI_) +
  a short **facilitator guide PDF** — that one addition moves it from a $18–30 toy to a professional
  tool. Physical (print-on-demand pipeline exists) **and** digital license.
- **Price: $39–69 retail; bulk/per-seat for orgs (10+ decks).** COGS ~$8–12 physical / ~$0 digital.

### Tier 2 — "WhoCards for Teams" — SaaS — _the low-touch tools pole; the real recurring business_

- **This is exactly issue #45**, re-justified as the monetization layer: org accounts,
  company-branded + **custom decks**, manager-authored **custom questions**, a **monthly fresh
  AI-conversation pack** (auto-generated via the question-gen prompt already built), saved
  favorites, and a lightweight **"what the team surfaced" recap** view.
- **Price: $5–10/seat/mo, or $1–3k/yr per team/org.** Gross margin ~85–90% (engine exists). This is
  the compounding, defensible core.

### Tier 3 — Facilitated team session — _the high-touch services pole; "me or a coworker"_

- A packaged **~half-day session** run live by Avi (then a coworker, then certified facilitators)
  using the deck + a session runbook. Purpose isn't scale — it's **cash now + case studies + logos**
  that sell Tiers 1–2.
- **Price: land $3.5–6k/session** (comps: half/full-day AI workshops $3–12k; exec $5–20k). Delivered
  by a non-founder facilitator at ~$1.5–2k cost ⇒ ~50%+ margin and it **stops being capped by Avi's
  calendar.**

### Tier 4 — Facilitator network / programs — _scale the high-touch pole_

- **Train-the-facilitator** certification + **multi-session programs / retainers** (e.g. a quarterly
  cadence for an org going through a rollout). Turns one-off sessions into recurring services and
  removes the founder-time ceiling.
- **Price: $8–20k multi-session programs** (comps: structured cohorts $8–40k); certification fee +
  rev-share on facilitator-delivered sessions.

**Ladder logic:** Tier 0 is a funnel, Tier 1 is a tripwire, Tier 2 is the business, Tier 3 is the
proof + margin, Tier 4 removes the scaling ceiling on Tier 3.

## 6. Who buys (ICP) & the buying motion

- **Primary (top-down):** mid-market orgs (200–2,000 ppl) actively rolling out AI where a
  **People/L&D leader, an "AI champion," or a Chief-AI/transformation lead** owns adoption and is
  **failing on the human side**. Their pain, in their words: _"My AI rollout is stalling and I can't
  tell if it's resistance, fear, or just confusion."_
- **Secondary (bottom-up / PLG):** an individual **team manager** who feels the tension and wants a
  20-minute tool _this week_. Enters via Tier 0/1, pulls us into the org → Tier 2/3.

**Motion = PLG-led, sales-assisted.** Learn the buyer through Tier 0→1 self-serve before committing
heavy sales muscle to Tier 2/3 (B2B contracts are new muscle for WhoCards — start light).

## 7. Business model, unit economics & projections

**Revenue mix by pole:**

- Low-touch (Tier 0–2): recurring SaaS + deck sales — high margin, compounding, slow to start.
- High-touch (Tier 3–4): services — lumpy, high-ticket, cash-now, founder-time-limited until Tier 4.

**Unit economics (illustrative):**

| Line                         | Price         | Cost                | Gross margin | Ceiling           |
| ---------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------- | ------------ | ----------------- |
| Tier 1 deck (digital)        | $49           | ~$0                 | ~100%        | none              |
| Tier 1 deck (physical POD)   | $49           | ~$10                | ~80%         | none              |
| Tier 2 SaaS                  | $5–10/seat/mo | ~$0.5/seat          | ~85–90%      | none              |
| Tier 3 session (Avi)         | $4k           | ~$0 cash (his time) | ~time-bound  | Avi's calendar    |
| Tier 3 session (facilitator) | $4k           | ~$1.5–2k            | ~50–60%      | # of facilitators |

**12-month revenue scenarios** (planning ranges, B2B-specific; consistent with growth-doc §5):

| Scenario             | What happens                                           | Y1 revenue                                                                                |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **A — Status quo**   | Tier 0 ships, no active B2B selling                    | ~$0–10k (incidental deck sales)                                                           |
| **B — Conservative** | Ship Tier 0–1, sell Tier 3 by hand, seed Tier 2 pilots | **~$40–100k** — services-led (6–12 sessions × $4k) + early SaaS ARR ($10–40k) + decks     |
| **C — Aggressive**   | PLG flywheel turns; Tier 2 compounds; Tier 4 begins    | **~$250–500k** — 30+ sessions, 50+ team licenses, first facilitator-delivered engagements |

**Leading indicators (which scenario you're in):**

1. Tier 0 **email capture rate** at end-of-deck (target >15%).
2. **Tier 3 sessions booked/quarter** (services demand is the fastest signal of real budget).
3. **Tier 2 pilot → paid conversion**.
4. **Organic (Google + AI-search) visits/mo** to `/ai-at-work` trending up by M3.
5. **B2B email → call conversion** on the landing.

**The honest read:** first real revenue is almost certainly **services (Tier 3)**, sold by hand from
Tier 0 leads. Services fund and prove the SaaS. Don't wait for Tier 2 to be built to start
selling Tier 3 — sell the session with the free deck you already have.

## 12. Two structural additions — the moderation layer + the TV/shared-screen surface

Two pieces turn this from a deck business into a **platform**.

### (a) The moderation / facilitation cockpit — cross-cutting, the actual platform

"Moderation tools across everything" is **not a feature — it's the layer that ties the ladder
together.** It does three strategic jobs:

1. **Productizes the human service.** A facilitator with a cockpit delivers Tier 3 _repeatably_;
   a manager with the cockpit needs no facilitator at all. It's what makes Tier 3 → Tier 4
   (scale) possible and what lets Tier 2 stand alone.
2. **Makes it enterprise-buyable.** Anonymous submission, controlled reveal, consent, data
   retention, safety flags = **psychological safety productized** — and the security/HR/legal
   sign-off that turns a $49 deck into a $10k contract. (Psychological safety is _the_ documented
   unlock for AI adoption; this productizes it directly.)
3. **Feeds the data asset.** Structured responses → the "what the team surfaced" recap → an
   aggregate benchmark (see §13.3).

**The category is real and funded.** Comps: **Slido / Mentimeter** — moderation on a shared screen,
phones respond, native Zoom/Teams (Slido acquired by Cisco; Mentimeter from ~$12/presenter/mo, Slido
$150–1,800/yr) — and **Culture Amp / Lattice / 15Five** — employee-engagement/pulse software, a
**~$1.3B market in 2026 growing ~16%/yr.** WhoCards' unoccupied position:
**"Slido meets Culture Amp, for honest AI/change conversations"** — with emotional depth, 14
languages, and AI question-gen that none of them have.

### (b) The TV / shared-screen surface — the "board"

The **Jackbox pattern applied to work: phones are the moderation remotes, the big screen is the
shared board.** Three jobs:

- **Facilitation surface** — conference room, offsite, projector; the natural home for the
  cockpit's output.
- **Ambient-retention surface** — office/lobby "question of the day," digital-signage style.
- **New distribution channel** — Apple TV / Google TV / Roku / Samsung / LG stores are far less
  crowded than mobile. Plus a **consumer wedge** (family game night on the TV) that feeds brand
  top-of-funnel into the B2B.

**Scope discipline:** don't ship five native TV apps. Start with a **cast / web shared-screen** mode
or lean on the **video-call surface (#180)** — a question shown in-call _is_ a shared screen without
screen-share — then one native app (Apple TV) only if signal warrants.

### The unifying insight — the parked epics are surfaces of this one layer

| Surface                           | Epic           | Role                                             |
| --------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Mobile / web `<Play>`             | shipped        | self-serve + PLG                                 |
| **TV / shared screen**            | new            | in-room facilitation + ambient + consumer wedge  |
| **Video-call (question-in-call)** | **#180**       | remote/hybrid facilitation surface               |
| **Chat (Slack/Discord/Teams)**    | **#179**       | the **async** pulse surface (submit over a week) |
| Events / QR                       | growth Lever 2 | live top-of-funnel                               |

**One moderation cockpit, six surfaces.** That's the platform and the moat a card company (WNRS)
can't copy — and it re-frames #179/#180 from standalone bets into **surfaces of the AI-at-work
platform**, which is how to decide them.

## 13. Business models beyond the ladder (ranked)

The ladder (§5) is product-led SaaS + productized services. Additional models the assets + the
moderation/TV layer unlock, ranked by near-term conviction:

1. **Managed "AI Pulse" retainer — _bet: now._** Tier 3 made recurring: a monthly/quarterly managed
   cadence ("we run your AI check-in; here's the sentiment report"). Recurring services revenue,
   higher retention than one-off; the cockpit + async chat surface (#179) make it deliverable at
   scale. **The missing rung between one-off service (Tier 3) and self-serve SaaS (Tier 2).**
2. **B2B2B licensing / white-label / embed — _bet: explore early._** Embed the engine + moderation
   widget inside tools the buyer already owns — HR platforms (Culture Amp, Lattice, 15Five,
   Workleap), LMS (Go1, SAP), EAP/benefits, consultancies. "Powered by WhoCards." The moderation
   layer is what makes it embeddable; distribution arbitrage over building our own base.
3. **Data / benchmarking — "The AI Sentiment Index" — _bet: free PR/lead-gen now, paid later._**
   Aggregate (anonymized, consented) recap data into a benchmark ("your team's AI anxiety vs
   market"). Data-network-effect moat + content/PR engine + eventual paid leadership insights —
   Culture Amp's entire moat is benchmarks. Needs scale + consent first.
4. **Sponsored / underwritten distribution — _bet: opportunistic._** Generalize the proven
   sponsor-funded events model: AI vendors underwrite free check-ins because _willingness drives
   their capability sales_. Co-branded "Copilot Check-In, powered by WhoCards." Someone else pays
   for distribution.
5. **Two-sided marketplace (facilitators + decks) — _bet: later / optionality._** Take-rate
   connecting orgs ↔ certified facilitators (beyond Tier 4), and UGC decks authored/sold by
   experts. Network effects + long-tail moat; chicken-and-egg, needs the base first.
6. **Individual credential — "Certified AI Conversation Facilitator" — _bet: later._** L&D pros pay
   for LinkedIn-able credentials; the certified become an evangelist army. Adjacent revenue +
   distribution.

_Honorable mentions:_ corporate-gifting / onboarding-kit bulk decks; budget-line positioning under
change-mgmt/DEI/wellbeing; EAP/benefits channel for the "AI anxiety = wellbeing" buyer.

### The bigger reframe — AI-at-work is the wedge, not the ceiling

The moderation layer + multi-surface + AI question-gen is a general engine for **any hard workplace
conversation** — layoffs, RTO, restructuring, post-merger, DEI, retros, feedback. AI-at-work is the
timed **beachhead** (hot budget _now_); the durable platform is **"honest conversations at work."**
Same tech, far larger TAM. Sequence: win the AI wedge, then expand horizontally.

**Focus discipline:** don't build all six models or six surfaces at once. The concrete near-term bet
is **the moderation cockpit + one shared-screen surface + the managed-retainer motion.**
Licensing / data / marketplace / credential are optionality the platform _unlocks_, not a backlog.
